KORStockScan working rules:

## 1. Required Context And Ownership

- At task start, read [Plan Rebase](docs/plan-korStockScanPerformanceOptimization.rebase.md) §1–§8 and the current KST daily `docs/checklists/YYYY-MM-DD-stage2-todo-checklist.md` objective and mandatory rules. Reuse unchanged session context. Plan Rebase owns strategy principles, active/observe/OFF state, data eligibility and rollback guards; the checklist owns executable OPEN work, stable IDs, schedules and acceptance.
- Resolve changing policy values, dates, symbol sets, market mappings, code locations and limits from their owning contract and actual producer/consumer before use. Links locate owners; a filename, old example or remembered default is not current authority. For historical evidence, use its original dated contract. Report missing/conflicting context; do not substitute an old checklist or guess a permissive default. Block only work dependent on the unresolved contract and continue independent authorized work.
- Read only task-relevant linked sections; do not recursively load audits or archives. Read the [session pointer](docs/plan-korStockScanPerformanceOptimization.prompt.md) only on request or when the document map is unclear. Completed old items are evidence, not current owners.
- Distinguish document editing, read-only result review, and invoked operational monitoring/recovery. [Intraday](docs/intraday-monitoring-task-instructions.md) and [postclose](docs/postclose-tuning-result-review-task-instructions.md) monitoring include their bounded source-only repairs; editing or quoting instructions does not invoke them. Trading-process transitions, manual environment/lock/provider/threshold changes and orders require authority covering that action. Reuse existing explicit approval within its scope and validity; do not require the user to repeat it for each stage.

## 2. Decision And Authority Boundaries

- Apply Plan Rebase's objective, family-specific decision metrics, canary rules and source-quality contracts. Before tuning or promotion, verify both the clean-data boundary and any narrower family-refresh window against their native owners. Earlier data retains only its permitted audit/custody/rollback roles. Do not copy a baseline date or extend one family's metric exception to other decisions.
- Exclude identifiable bad rows/windows. Whole-input blocking requires missing/invalid preflight, failed isolation or unidentifiable high-volume contract loss; unknown-token warnings alone do not block all input.
- Realized PnL requires completed trades and valid profit results under the owning schema; missing costs/outcomes remain null, never zero or gross EV. Separate full/partial fills, real/sim/probe/counterfactual, owner and venue/session. Verify the declared selection metric separately from realized economics, including required rolling/cumulative or post-apply evidence. Preserve approved overrides, expiry and custody; do not add an economic proof gate to an explicitly authorized initial adoption.
- For post-decision non-entry, trace the actual decision, terminal reason and downstream consumer using the owning attribution contract. Repair source consistency before tuning thresholds; do not force new or unknown reasons into a remembered fixed taxonomy.
- Preserve manual custody, operator vetoes, historical exit ownership and hard safety. Verify active and permanently retired owners against Plan Rebase and native admission/journal contracts, including renamed or alternate entry paths. Permanently retired execution, workers, UI, collection, research and publication cannot be restored through rollback or old configuration. Retired holdings remain operator-managed: Main must not adopt or sell them, their residual quantities/intents do not block Main, and flat reconciliation is not a startup requirement. Never fabricate flat/fill/terminal receipts.
- Preserve hard/protect/emergency, stale/conflict, price freshness and broker/account/order/quantity/cooldown guards. Report, test or deployment success does not grant policy, provider, bot-state, cap, order or safety-change authority.

### Market Identity And Natural Acceptance

- Before market-specific analysis, repair or acceptance, identify five distinct identities: session, policy/effective venue cohort, market-data item/route, requested/effective broker route, and broker-verified actual execution venue. Use the [Kiwoom data contract](docs/kiwoom-api-data-contract.md) for upstream semantics and locate the selected session, watch/attachment, route-default and submit consumers for current mappings. Check historical records against their dated implementation.
- Verify field precedence, explicit overrides, supported aliases, source epoch/clock and session guards end to end. Preserve an explicit valid broker route through preparation, retry, AI context and submit; apply the current authorized default only to unresolved requests. Never infer a route or physical venue from the clock, a legacy label or a cohort assignment. Different candle and executable-quote sources may coexist when their consumer permits it; do not relabel them to force equality.
- Actual exchange attribution requires a matching native execution receipt and explicit, consistent exchange evidence. Preserve unknown/conflicting states and raw provenance; do not invent undocumented semantics or impute absent optional fields. An operator assignment is not broker verification.
- Natural acceptance must exercise the repaired branch with its exact session, source item and route, bound to the actual release/PID and policy. Evidence from another route or branch cannot close it. Otherwise keep the existing checklist owner OPEN with the exact next window. Offline tests and future-clock checks are not natural observations. Diagnostic acceptance needs no real order/fill; venue, fill and economics claims need their own native evidence. Do not reroute orders or add subscriptions to manufacture acceptance.

## 5. Review And Completion

- Every modification follows `implementation -> self review -> supplemental fixes -> re-review -> targeted validation -> result report`; use `$korstockscan-review-gate` when available. Check producer/consumer consistency, missing/stale inputs, silent failures and authority leaks. Fix in-scope findings; identify actions requiring new authority. Runtime follow-up occurs only after the review and relevant checks pass and §1 authority covers it. Error diagnosis/recovery follows §6.
- **Postclose completion includes the next Main startup unless the user limits scope or time.** Follow the [end-to-end acceptance contract](docs/postclose-tuning-result-review-task-instructions.md#14-장후-전체-흐름의-기본-완료-기준) through calculation, compression/cleanup, finalization, next-session PREOPEN and actual Main policy/PID consumption with current observer/health evidence. Within existing authority, continue repair, integration/deployment and regeneration through the intended last consumer. Preparation-only scope ends at verified readiness, leaving actual startup acceptance OPEN. A repaired run is recovered completion; keep unassisted acceptance OPEN through the next scheduled full run. Tests, stage completion and prepared startup checks do not close natural acceptance.
- Future windows or authority/evidence blockers stay under the same current checklist owner with exact dates, next action and closure test. Preserve dependencies through their last consumer. Distinguish code review, deployment, source generation, policy selection, preparation, actual PID consumption, natural acceptance and cost-adjusted economics. Reopen completed work only for new defects, changed contracts or mandatory handoff failure; absent samples alone do not reopen code cleanup.
- Before creating/moving code or tests, inspect package ownership, nearby callers and enforced location rules. Prefer existing role packages; `src/engine` root is closed to new Python modules by default. Exceptions require an intentional engine-root allowlist test update and written ownership reason. Recheck the current layout and its owning test before applying this placement rule; update them together if that interface moves. Explain why an existing owner cannot be extended before adding a module, producer or validator.
- **Replacement/removal is part of completion.** In the existing review/PR/result, map affected old files/functions/dispatches to a replacement or `none`, marking `removed`, `consolidated`, `retained` or `deferred` with caller/consumer evidence. Inspect dynamic imports, CLI/shell callers and registry/schedule entries; text-search absence, moves, OFF flags or wrappers alone do not prove removal. Remove displaced implementations, registrations and duplicated checks once consumers migrate.
- Retained code needs a current consumer and reason. Wrapper modules must be temporary compatibility surfaces, forwarding to one implementation only while a named caller needs them. Deferred removal needs an existing owner, checkable trigger and closure test; unexplained old paths block removal completion. A documented external dependency may permit functional closure with removal explicitly deferred. Preserve necessary historical readers and immutable evidence without restoring retired authority.
- Record baseline commit/scope and additions/deletions separately for non-test source, tests and docs, distinguishing moves and unrelated changes. Explain net growth and changes in owners/branches/entrypoints. No negative-line quota: moving/minifying is not simplification. Reuse fixtures/parameterization while preserving distinct failure coverage; do not add validators solely to enforce this rule.
- Run `git diff --check`. Python changes use relevant pytest and compile checks; wrappers use `bash -n` and affected contract tests. Documents/checklists use link/owner/authority checks and `PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python -m src.engine.sync_docs_backlog_to_project --print-backlog-only --limit 500`. Verify supported tooling before use. Read-only explanation needs no implementation loop; document-only work needs no trading suites, provider calls or regeneration.
- Report `decision -> evidence -> next action`: functional result, replacement/removal disposition, validation/skips and blockers with owner/artifact/closure test. Do not collapse the acceptance states above into a single success claim.

## 6. Error Resolution Procedure

Apply this procedure within the authorized scope, including recurrent alerts. Temporary containment must name its owner, expiry/removal trigger and remaining causal repair; it is not root-cause closure.

1. **Pin the incident.** Record failing command/check, expected versus actual result, first failure/last valid evidence, date/scope/IDs and source generation. Check each timestamp's producer-defined timezone and serialization; interpret offset-free time only under that field's verified contract. Verify actual producer/consumer, selected release and relevant PID/start ticks; distinguish old-release alerts from current failure. Preserve original bytes/attempts and group related alerts under the existing owner without losing distinct identities.
2. **Prove the first broken contract.** Trace collection/trigger through queue, writer, parser/index, publication, consumer and detector. Identify the responsible predicate and why invalid state was produced or accepted. Test the causal hypothesis with a bounded reproducer, regression or exact failure evidence; an alert string/downstream exception alone is insufficient. Keep unproved hypotheses explicit and continue diagnosis rather than changing unrelated code.
3. **Classify before repairing.** Distinguish active producer/consumer failure, detector misclassification, expected wait/valid-empty/guard behavior and irreversible historical loss against the owning scope/deadline. Missing evidence is neither zero economics nor success. Exclusion contains bad rows but does not repair their producer. Correct a detector's interpretation without altering valid data to satisfy it.
4. **Repair the existing owner.** Fix the earliest violation and affected direct consumers for both present and future inputs. Inspect shared callers, retries, partial writes, caches and generation transitions. For permanent source/master removal, trace normal/fallback intake, startup rebuild, persistence and identity aliases; prevent re-intake/updates while preserving required historical/custody evidence. Apply §5 removal rules. Manual artifact/status/hash edits, forced completion, stale PASS reuse, broader exception swallowing, warning suppression or guard/timeout/size-limit relaxation alone are not repairs; do not add a wrapper/validator/fallback merely to bypass the defect.
5. **Bound recovery and resource work.** Before rerunning, name the change, recoverable evidence, minimal dependency range and stop/progress condition. Map changed source/code generations to indexes, summaries, strict checks, preparation and terminal receipts, resolving aliases. Verify reused predecessors and rebuild invalidated dependencies in order. No unchanged full rerun or endless retry after deterministic failure or irreversible loss. After deployment workflow failure, prove the first broken contract and validate the remaining sequence offline before another live attempt; another error message, expanded accepted-error list or increased retry depth is not causal repair. For growth/backlog, fix the existing writer-index/checkpoint-manifest-consumer path using bounded/incremental reads with identity/coverage checks. Measure bytes, growth versus throughput, lag and relevant resource usage; larger budgets and repeated whole-source scans are insufficient. Bound any one-time migration and make it resumable. After quarantine/replacement/compression/rotation, verify source identity and index coverage before the first dependent reader. Reuse native preparation for each consumer's window after its last writer; early narrow preparation cannot replace a later wider or updated one.
6. **Review and validate the cause.** Apply §5. Preserve a regression that fails on the original defect and passes after repair, plus missing/stale/conflicting/new-generation and hard-guard negative cases. Exercise applicable clock formats, date/phase boundaries and failure unwind. Use existing fixtures and offline mocks without live provider/order calls. Verify affected producer and consumer together, not only a helper/alert formatter. Document-only changes retain document-only validation.
7. **Close through actual consumption.** Regenerate only invalidated dependencies under existing authority, verify native semantics/date/scope/hash, then check the intended last consumer and original failure. Use the stage-specific procedures and §5 completion boundary; exit zero, publication, alert absence or restart alone is insufficient. After terminal sealing, prefer read-only generation/health-receipt verification. Overwriting a bound artifact invalidates that proof and requires native downstream resealing; never rerun merely for another PASS. Future-due scheduling is not proof of preceding completion. Diagnostic repair requires no unrelated fill, positive EV or promotion floor.
8. **Handle recurrence without hiding failures.** Irrecoverable historical gaps may remain excluded/null with exact IDs, reasons and hashes under their native contract. Move them to historical diagnostics only after verifying the current producer/consumer and classification. Test continued detection of new loss, invalidated bindings and unknown provenance. Notification deduplication does not resolve the incident: retain failure, owner and next action; surface changed impact or failed recovery. Current recurrence reopens the causal hypothesis/acceptance under the same owner; unchanged historical exclusion does not. Record `cause -> repair -> regression -> consumer evidence -> residual loss/next action` in the existing review/checklist, naming the future-input prevention contract and remaining acceptance. Do not repeat a workaround or create parallel tracking.
